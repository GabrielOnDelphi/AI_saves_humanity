# Proposal · Beyond Democracy — Optimize the Target, Not the Proxy

**Status:** Draft for debate · **Last updated:** 2026-06-29 · Reframes the *goal* that every governance proposal here aims at. Sits beside the spine ([proposal 00](../00-how-change-happens/)); closely tied to [#05 Governance](../../problems/05-governance-democracy/), [#01 Inequality](../../problems/01-economic-inequality/), and the [founding political-system proposal](../political-system/).

> Owner's challenge: *"I want to work around democracy. Even if it is seen as the golden standard, it could be a false standard… until today democracy did nothing special. In my view it failed. Maybe because it was the wrong type of democracy or it was implemented wrongly?"* This folder takes that seriously — and corrects the half that the data contradicts while building on the half that is exactly right.

## 1. Democracy is a means, not an end

Nobody wants "elections" the way they want to be safe, fed, free, and able to remove a ruler who abuses them. **Democracy was never the goal — it was the best-known *mechanism* for delivering two things that are:** *(A) power held accountable to ordinary people*, and *(B) lives actually going well.* Treating the mechanism as the goal is a category error, and it has a name: **Goodhart's Law** — *when a measure becomes the target, it stops being a good measure.* We started grading countries on the proxy ("do you hold elections? what's your democracy-index score?") and stopped checking the target. A country can now **pass the proxy and fail the target** — and that is precisely what the evidence below shows.

## 2. What democracy actually does — and fails to do (the evidence)

**It does something real, and it is mostly invisible — because it is an *absence*.** Democracy is not a turbocharger for the average outcome; it is **catastrophe-insurance** that crushes the *downside*:

- **No famine has ever occurred in a functioning democracy with a free press** (Amartya Sen) — broadly supported by later cross-country work. The deadliest famines in history (the Great Leap Forward, the Holodomor) were all under autocracy. ([ThePrint summary of Sen](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/amartya-sen-said-no-democracy-with-free-press-has-had-major-famines/))
- **~4× lower growth volatility:** growth-rate variance was ~5.5 across democratic country-decades vs ~23.1 across autocratic ones (1990–2009); democracies suffer fewer economic collapses. ([*A business case for democracy*, Democratization 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13510347.2021.1940965))
- **+~20% GDP per capita over 25 years** after democratizing, with the causal direction tested carefully. ([Acemoglu, Naidu, Restrepo & Robinson, *JPE* 2019](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/700936))
- **The top 10 happiest countries are all democracies** — zero autocracies. ([World Happiness Report 2025](https://data.worldhappiness.report/table))

So the owner's "democracy did nothing special" is **wrong as stated** — and the trap it leads toward ("try a strongman") is the most dangerous move on the board: the worst human outcomes cluster under authoritarianism. **Whatever we design must keep the insurance.**

**But it fails at the target it was supposed to serve — and here the owner is right.** The decisive finding is [Gilens & Page (2014)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/testing-theories-of-american-politics-elites-interest-groups-and-average-citizens/62327F513959D0A304D4893B382B992B): across **1,779 U.S. policy decisions**, the preferences of the average citizen have a **near-zero independent effect** on policy, while economic elites and business groups drive it. Their verdict: the U.S. fits **"Economic-Elite Domination,"** *not* "Majoritarian Electoral Democracy." The country passes the proxy (elections happen) and fails the target (the people don't actually rule). That is the [money→power loop of #01](../../problems/01-economic-inequality/), measured.

**And the proxy isn't even necessary for half the target:** Singapore and Qatar out-earn democratic Norway in GDP per capita ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/gdp-per-capita-vs-electoral-democracy-index)). They are rentier/city-state special cases that deliver (B) prosperity while failing (A) accountability — and they still carry the authoritarian tail-risk. They prove the proxy and the target are **not the same thing**, which is the whole point.

## 3. The target, defined

Replace "is it a democracy?" with two measurable questions:

- **(A) Accountable power** — can ordinary people *constrain and replace* those who rule, and stop abuse before it becomes catastrophe? This is the insurance (it prevents famines, purges, looting). Free press, removability, voice.
- **(B) Flourishing** — are lives actually going well? Prosperity, health, learning, freedom, meaning. The repo's own [problem list](../../README.md) is essentially the flourishing scorecard.

Success = hitting **A + B**, by whatever mechanism. "Democratic" is then an empirical question (does this mechanism raise A and B?), not a badge awarded for holding elections.

## 4. Mechanisms that hit the target *and* resist capture

If the disease is **capture** (money buying representatives — §2), the cure is mechanisms that are hard to buy. This is not hypothetical: the [OECD documented ~600 representative deliberative processes](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/innovative-citizen-participation-and-new-democratic-institutions_339306da-en.html) ("the deliberative wave").

1. **Sortition / citizens' assemblies** — decision-makers chosen *randomly*, like juries. *For:* you cannot lobby or fund a panel you can't predict; it is structurally capture-resistant. Worked example: Ireland's randomly-selected assemblies broke decades of deadlock and led directly to the 2018 abortion and 2015 same-sex-marriage referendums — [four assembly recommendations → referendums since 2012, three → constitutional amendments](https://electoral-reform.org.uk/the-irish-abortion-referendum-how-a-citizens-assembly-helped-to-break-years-of-political-deadlock/). *Against:* legitimacy ("I didn't elect them"); scales to *assemblies*, not obviously to a national executive.
2. **Money out of politics** — attacks the Gilens-Page channel head-on. *For:* targets the exact mechanism of capture. *Against:* where capture is constitutionalized (e.g. U.S. *Citizens United*), it may be self-locking — see transition path.
3. **Free press + radical transparency** — the famine-prevention core; keep it, fund it ([information-integrity](../information-integrity/)). *Against:* itself under attack in backsliding states ([#14](../../problems/14-democratic-backsliding/)).
4. **A constitutional rights floor + rule of law** — the insurance against the strongman temptation this proposal must never invite. Non-negotiable.
5. **Liquid democracy / delegation** — vote directly or delegate your vote to someone you trust, revocably. Informed accountability *without* disenfranchising anyone — the goal the [founding proposal](../political-system/) was really reaching for (not vote-weighting).
6. **Outcome-accountability** — judge a government on measured movement in the flourishing dashboard (§6), not on whether it held an election.

## 5. The hard constraint — keep the catastrophe-insurance

This proposal is **not** "ditch democracy for efficient technocracy." The verified tail-risk asymmetry (§2) forbids it: removability, free press, and voice are what prevent the worst outcomes, and a benevolent-dictator model throws exactly those away. Sortition + rights floor + free press *preserve* the insurance while removing the capturable money-election layer. Any mechanism that improves (B) flourishing by sacrificing (A) accountability is rejected here on the evidence, not on sentiment.

> **Companion — the two fastest ways to lose the insurance:** [`speed-vs-reversibility.md`](speed-vs-reversibility.md) tackles the two proposals people reach for when democracy feels too slow — *a benevolent dictator* and *instant direct voting on every issue* — and shows they are the same bet (buy speed by removing the brakes) and fail for the same reason (Popper: the question isn't "who should rule?" but "how do we remove bad rule cheaply?"). Includes the straight answer to *"but a leader can turn bad under democracy too — see Trump,"* the adverse-selection objection (*"decent people don't run"*), and *"isn't speed important?"*

> **Companion — frontier mechanisms & the math: [`frontier-mechanisms.md`](frontier-mechanisms.md).** Past the elections-only menu — futarchy, quadratic voting, AI-augmented deliberation (vTaiwan), a permanent sortition chamber (East Belgium), default-sunset, exit — and why the impossibility theorems (Arrow, Gibbard–Satterthwaite, McKelvey–Schofield) mean the goal is a layered *robust removal stack*, not a perfect system. Also corrects "democracy is 2,000 years old": at full inclusion it's ~80.

## 6. Metrics — outcomes, not procedures

- **Accountability (A):** can power be removed and constrained? (Binary insurance test, plus press-freedom and rule-of-law indices.)
- **Responsiveness:** a Gilens-Page-style test — does the *median citizen's* preference predict policy *independent of* elite preference? (The cleanest "captured or not" measure.)
- **Flourishing (B):** the repo's primary-source dashboard — real income of the bottom half, health access, learning, mental health ([#11](../../problems/11-mental-health-social/)), freedom.
- **The proxy-vs-target gap:** high democracy score *but* low responsiveness = captured. Publishing this gap is the smallest first move (§8).

## 7. Objections (steelman against this proposal)

- **"You've just redefined 'liberal democracy done right' and called it new."** Partly fair — much of §4 is what liberal democracy was *supposed* to be. The novelty is not the mechanisms; it's **changing what we measure and optimize**: the target, not the proxy. Optimize a captured proxy and you get more capture.
- **"Measuring 'flourishing' hands someone the power to define it — a Ministry of Happiness, the worst capture surface of all."** The strongest objection. Mitigation: flourishing must be a **plural, transparent, hard-to-fake dashboard** of primary-source indicators (the repo's own discipline), never one authority's single index — and (A) accountability is the check on whoever compiles it. If we can't measure flourishing without capture, we fall back to (A) plus the existing problem-metrics and say so.
- **"Sortition has no electoral mandate; it erodes the consent that makes rule legitimate."** Real. Response: it's **additive** — keep elections for legitimacy where they work, insert sortition/assemblies where capture is worst (and ratify their output by referendum, the Irish model). Consent and capture-resistance are both satisfied.
- **"Rich autocracies deliver (B) without (A) — doesn't your model have to score Singapore a partial success?"** Yes, and it should say so honestly: Singapore passes on B, fails on A, and carries tail-risk. The model's claim is that **A is the insurance you only miss when it's gone** — which is exactly why a snapshot flatters the autocrat.

## 8. Transition path (who blocks this · which door · smallest first move)

- **Who blocks it:** the captured elites who *like* a proxy that passes while the target fails — buyable elections are their moat; plus voters' genuine attachment to "we voted," which sortition unsettles.
- **Which door:** **Door 1** (money-out and citizens' assemblies can be adopted incrementally and sub-nationally, as Ireland and ~600 OECD cases show) and **Door 3** ([a new polity](../country-and-citizenship/) can write target-optimization in from day one, with no incumbents to defeat).
- **Smallest first move:** **change the scoreboard.** Publish a *target-vs-proxy* index that pairs each country's democracy score with (i) a responsiveness measure and (ii) a flourishing dashboard. It needs no one's permission, costs almost nothing, and it makes capture *visible* — and you cannot fix what the official metric is built to hide. Everything else in this folder becomes arguable only once the gap is on the table.

## 9. Open questions

- Can flourishing (B) be measured **without** building the capture surface this whole project warns against?
- Does sortition scale to a national **executive**, or is its honest ceiling assemblies + referendum ratification?
- Is money-in-politics capture **self-locking** (constitutionalized), so that Door 1 can't reach it and only Door 3 can?
- How do we weight **A vs B** when they diverge (the Singapore case)? Is accountability lexically prior because it's the insurance — or is that a value choice we should state openly rather than smuggle into a metric?
