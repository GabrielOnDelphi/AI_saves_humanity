# Proposal · 00 · How Change Actually Happens (The Transition Problem)

**Status:** Draft for debate · **Last updated:** 2026-06-29 · The spine that ties the rest together. Relates to **every** problem folder, and especially [#01 Inequality](../../problems/01-economic-inequality/) and [#05 Governance](../../problems/05-governance-democracy/).

> This project had two-thirds of a machine. The [`problems/`](../../problems/) folders say *what's wrong*. The other proposals say *what's better*. Neither answered the only question that has ever mattered: **how do you get from here to there, when the people who must approve the fix are the people the fix threatens?** That gap is why most well-meaning reform documents — including, until now, this one — change nothing. This folder is the missing third gear.

---

## 1. The meta-problem: every good reform dies at the transition

Take any solution in this repo: progressive wealth tax (#01), money out of politics (#05), universal health coverage (#02), carbon pricing (#04). None of them are mysteries. Serious people have proposed each, in detail, for **decades to a century**. They don't fail because we don't know the policy. They fail at the **transition** — the step where the proposal has to be enacted by, or wrested from, the people who profit from the current arrangement and who control the levers of enactment.

The repo already half-admitted this. The founding political proposal buries it as *Open Question #4*:

> "How do you transition from a captured status quo to any of these without the incumbents blocking it?"

That is not open question #4. **That is the whole game.** Everything else is a wish addressed to a benevolent implementer who does not exist. The pattern that produced the world we have:

```
good policy  →  threatens the powerful  →  the powerful control the enactment mechanism  →  policy dies
                                          ↑___________________ repeat for 200 years __________________|
```

If a proposal cannot name who would block it and how that block is overcome, it is not a plan. It is a prayer.

## 2. Two reasons "what we tried until now" didn't work

### 2.1 We keep pulling the weakest levers

Donella Meadows ranked the *places to intervene in a system* by leverage. From **weakest to strongest** (abridged):

| Leverage | Intervention | Where this repo's old solutions sat |
|---|---|---|
| Weakest | **Parameters** — tax rates, subsidies, numbers | ← almost everything: tax %, antitrust tweaks |
| ↓ | Feedback loops, information flows | a little (transparency) |
| ↑ | **Rules of the system** — incentives, constraints, *who gets to make the rules* | rarely touched |
| ↑ | **Goals of the system** | almost never |
| Strongest | **The paradigm** the system arises from | never |

Her point, in one line: *"People know intuitively where leverage points are… and they push in the wrong direction."* ([Meadows, *Leverage Points*, 1999](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/)) Arguing about the top marginal tax rate is a **parameter** fight — the lowest-leverage intervention there is. The money→power→money loop is a **rules-and-goals** problem: who is allowed to write the rules, and what the system is *for*. We have been fighting at the bottom of the leverage ladder and losing.

### 2.2 We assume a benevolent implementer

Almost every reform proposal silently assumes someone in power will, once persuaded by the evidence, adopt it. But the binding constraint was never **knowledge** — it was **power**. We have known the right policies for a century. The thing we have *not* solved is how to move power against its own will. A project called "AI Saves Humanity" that only produces better-argued policy is just adding to a 200-year-old pile of better-argued policy.

## 3. What actually shifts entrenched power (evidence, not vibes)

This is the part reform documents skip, so it's the part this project must lead with. The good news: it has been **measured**.

- **Nonviolent mass movements beat violent ones — about 2 to 1.** Erica Chenoweth and Maria Stephan built the NAVCO dataset of 323 campaigns, 1900–2006: nonviolent campaigns succeeded **~53%** of the time, violent ones **~26%**. ([Chenoweth & Stephan, *Why Civil Resistance Works*, 2011](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty-research/policy-topics/advocacy-social-movements/paths-resistance-erica-chenoweths-research))
- **The 3.5% threshold.** No campaign that reached the active, sustained participation of **3.5% of the population** failed to produce change. ([3.5% rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3.5%25_rule)) Read it correctly: 3.5% is a **marker of the kind of broad, committed participation that triggers the mechanism below — not a magic number you manufacture by counting heads at one rally.**

### Why it works — the mechanism (the load-bearing part)

Power is not a possession; it is a relationship that runs on the cooperation of many people — soldiers who follow orders, police who enforce, clerks who administer, workers who show up, citizens who comply. Nonviolence wins by **lowering the barrier to participation** (the old, the cautious, the respectable can join a march in a way they never could an armed cell), which builds larger and more diverse movements, which trigger the two things that actually bring regimes down:

1. **Loyalty shifts / security-force defections.** When the people ordered to crush the movement see their own neighbors, parents, and clergy in the crowd, they stop obeying. Chenoweth & Stephan find security-force defection raises a campaign's success probability by **~60%**. ([*Why Civil Resistance Works*, International Security, 2008 — PDF](https://www.belfercenter.org/sites/default/files/pantheon_files/files/publication/IS3301_pp007-044_Stephan_Chenoweth.pdf))
2. **The backfire effect.** Visible repression of unarmed people wins sympathy and third-party support and strips the regime of legitimacy — the opposite of what the repression was meant to do.

### The mechanism in the real world (verified cases)

- **Leipzig, 9 Oct 1989** — ~70,000 marched after police and army were authorized to use force. The security forces did not fire; "the police-state blinked first." The next Mondays drew 120,000, then 320,000; the Berlin Wall fell on 9 Nov. ([City of Leipzig](https://english.leipzig.de/services-and-administration/autumn-89/the-9th-october-1989); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monday_demonstrations_in_East_Germany)) — *loyalty shift / loss of coercive will.*
- **Manila (EDSA), Feb 1986** — Defense Minister Enrile and Gen. Ramos defected; hundreds of thousands formed human barricades; commanders refused orders to fire and the tanks stopped; Marcos fled. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution); [Origins, OSU](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/people-power-revolution-philippines-1986)) — *security-force defection.*
- **Belgrade, 5 Oct 2000** — Otpor's organizers cultivated the police *in advance*; on the day, several hundred thousand converged and the special police "obeyed orders but did not execute them." Milošević fell. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overthrow_of_Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87); [ICNC](https://www.nonviolent-conflict.org/otpor-struggle-democracy-serbia-1998-2000/)) — *defection, engineered on purpose.*
- **Selma, 7 Mar 1965** — state troopers beat marchers on the Edmund Pettus Bridge; ABC cut into a film premiere to show ~50 million Americans the footage; protests erupted in 80 cities; the Voting Rights Act was signed five months later. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_to_Montgomery_marches); [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/selma-bloody-sunday-attack-civil-rights-movement)) — *backfire.*

### Is it causal, or just correlation? (the honest answer)

The fair objection: movements don't *randomly* choose nonviolence, so maybe nonviolent campaigns simply arise where regimes are already weak. This is real and named in the literature. But the finding is **stronger than a bare correlation**: there is a specified mechanism (above), and the headline result survives two-stage models, country- and campaign-fixed effects, and newer event-study designs — under which the selection-driven estimates come out **small and statistically null**. It remains **observational** — no one runs a randomized trial on revolutions — and credible critics still dispute the methods ([example critique](https://statsandsociety.substack.com/p/why-why-civil-resistance-works-doesnt)). Honest verdict: **a mechanism-backed regularity that has survived several attempts to explain it away — not an iron law.**

### The crack I flagged, now measured: success is falling — and mostly self-inflicted

Nonviolent success **peaked at ~65% in the 1990s and fell to under 34% after 2010.** Chenoweth's own diagnosis is the important part: the main cause is **not** smarter repression — it's that **movements got worse.** Peak participation roughly halved (~2.7% → ~1.3% of the population); movements lean on one-off demonstrations instead of the higher-disruption tools (general strikes, sustained civil disobedience); digital organizing gathers crowds fast but builds shallow, fragile organizations; and the share of nominally nonviolent movements carrying a violent flank rose from ~30–35% to more than half. ([Chenoweth, *The Future of Nonviolent Resistance*, Journal of Democracy, 2020](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-future-of-nonviolent-resistance-2/))

That diagnosis is, perversely, **good news**: the decline is concentrated in the things movements *control* — depth of organization, choice of tactics, discipline. The method wasn't disproven; it's being executed worse. And it points to exactly where Door 4 (AI / information, [#10](../../problems/10-information-truth/)) bites: the new tools make **crowds easy and organization hard.**

## 4. The four doors (the strategy space)

If the transition is the real problem, there are exactly four doors out of a captured status quo. This project should work all four honestly, including the uncomfortable ones.

### Door 1 — Reform from within
Change the rules using the system's own mechanisms. *For:* legitimate, no violence, durable if it sticks. *Against:* it's the door the incumbents guard most heavily — capture (#05) exists precisely to keep this door shut. **Only works if paired with Door-3-style outside pressure (Section 3) that raises the cost of keeping it shut.**

### Door 2 — Defend what already works (stop the backsliding)
The cheapest win is not building a new democracy — it's **not losing the ones we have.** Democracies today rarely die by tank; they die slowly, legally, from the inside, at the hands of elected leaders who hollow out the referee institutions. → developed in **[#14 Democratic backsliding](../../problems/14-democratic-backsliding/)** *(you flagged the current U.S. trajectory as the live example)*. Defense is higher-leverage than it looks: a backslide erases decades of Door-1 progress in a few years.

### Door 3 — Exit and build new (a new country / polity)
If a system can't be reformed, build a clean one and let people choose it. *For:* no incumbents to defeat; you can design the rules right from day one. *Against:* serious — who is *excluded*, who funds it, the strong risk it becomes a tax haven / escape hatch for exactly the powerful actors who are problem #01. → developed in **[Founding a country](../country-and-citizenship/)**.

### Door 4 — The new variable: AI
Everything above was available to every reform movement in history. **One thing is new in 2026: AI changes the cost of the four things that always bottlenecked change** — coordination, transparency, expertise, and monitoring. That could hand citizens leverage they never had. It could *also* hand incumbents the most powerful tool of surveillance, manipulation, and control ever built — and concentrate civilization-scale power in whoever owns the model. It cuts both ways, hard. → developed in **[AI and power](../ai-and-power/)**. This is the wildcard that reshapes Doors 1–3, and it is the literal subject of this project's name.

## 5. Objections to this framing (steelman the critics)

- **"This is just activism dressed up."** No — it's the empirical finding that organized non-cooperation moves power and lone policy papers don't (Section 3). The alternative on offer is a century-long losing streak.
- **"Nonviolent resistance is failing now, so the evidence is stale."** Success did fall (~65% → <34% post-2010), but Chenoweth's data pins the main cause on movements *executing worse* — shallower participation, weaker organization, more violent flanks — not on the method failing (§3). The playbook degraded; it wasn't disproven. That's exactly why Door 4 (AI / information) is decisive: the new tools are what made crowds easy and organization shallow.
- **"Door 3 is a libertarian fantasy / a rich man's escape pod."** A strong objection, kept in full in that folder. A new country that just lets capital flee accountability makes #01 worse, not better.
- **"Door 4 is techno-utopian."** It is dual-use *by construction here* — the AI folder's core requirement is "no over-powerful owner." Treating AI as pure salvation would be the exact naïveté this project exists to avoid.
- **"You're telling people to take to the streets — that's dangerous."** The data says the *opposite* of the dangerous thing: nonviolent, broad, disciplined participation is both safer and more effective than the violent alternative people otherwise drift toward.

## 6. What this changes about the project (the new rule)

**Every proposal in this repo now needs a "Transition path" section** — the honest answer to: *who would block this, what door (1–4) gets past them, and what is the smallest first move that builds power toward it?* A proposal without that section is downgraded from "plan" to "wish," explicitly labeled.

This is the single highest-leverage edit to the project itself: it stops us from generating more un-implementable policy and forces every idea to confront power.

## 8. Open questions

- **3.5% — causation vs. correlation (now partly settled, still live):** the advantage is mechanism-backed and survives several selection tests (§3), but it's observational. The sharper open question is *operational:* 3.5% **active, sustained** participation is the marker — but no one can reliably manufacture it on demand. What actually *produces* that depth of commitment?
- **Is the post-2010 decline reversible?** Chenoweth's data says the main causes are self-inflicted (organization, tactics, discipline), which implies fixable. But is the drift to shallow digital mobilization a **choice** movements can reverse, or a **structural** feature of the attention economy ([#10](../../problems/10-information-truth/)) they can't escape?
- Can Door 1 (reform) ever work *without* Door 3 pressure (a credible exit/threat) behind it?
- Does Door 4 (AI) have a **stable** equilibrium where it empowers citizens, or does it inevitably tip toward whoever has the most compute? (The crux of the whole project's title.)
