# Can education-weighted voting be saved? The self-liquidating version, stress-tested

**Status:** Draft for debate · **Last updated:** 2026-06-30 · Companion to the [political-system proposal](README.md), which logged education-weighted voting (the owner's founding idea) with its standard rebuttal. The owner pushed back — *"why drop it so fast because 'a guy' said so? Every system failed at least once. Can we improve it?"* — and proposed a specific upgrade. He is right that the quick dismissal was too cheap. This page takes the **improved** version seriously and tests its *structure*, not its reputation.

## 1. The owner is right about the meta-point

"Drop it because Brennan dislikes it" is dismissal by authority. "Drop it because it failed once" is worse — **by that test we drop democracy too** (it elected would-be autocrats; see [#14](../../problems/14-democratic-backsliding/)). No system has a clean record. So the bar can't be *"did it ever fail."* The right bar: **does this version's failure mode survive the patch, and is that failure mode self-correcting or self-entrenching?** That test is applied evenly to democracy and to epistocracy below — and it is the only fair way to choose between them.

## 2. The owner's improved design — stated fairly

Not Mill's permanent extra votes for the educated. A **dynamic, self-liquidating loop**:

1. Vote weight rises with education.
2. The state **aggressively educates** the currently-uneducated ("push them quickly through school").
3. Newly-educated citizens now carry **more** weight — and an interest in education — so they vote to fund **more** education for their class.
4. → more education → more weight → more pro-education votes → … a **positive feedback loop.**
5. **Endpoint:** as education becomes universal, everyone reaches high weight, so the inequality **dissolves itself.** The hierarchy is a temporary scaffold, not a caste.

**This is a real improvement and deserves credit.** It fixes the worst feature of classic plural voting (permanence) and turns the vote into an *incentive engine for mass education.* The goal — better-informed decisions **and** universal education — is good. The question is only whether *vote-weighting* is the right engine for it.

## 3. Where it breaks — by structure, not by authority

**(a) Whoever defines "educated" and runs the schools controls the loop's sign — and the powerful are paid to flip it.** The loop assumes the people in power *want* to educate the masses and dilute their own relative weight. This project's entire upstream thesis ([#01](../../problems/01-economic-inequality/), [Gilens-Page](../../proposals/beyond-democracy/README.md)) is that power resists its own dilution. A captured elite would define "educated" to match its own children (elite credentials, a "civics test" it writes) and under-fund mass schooling — and then the **same loop runs in reverse**: high weight → policies that entrench the educated class → education stays scarce → the franchise gap *widens*. This isn't a hypothetical: **literacy tests were introduced by the powerful precisely to entrench exclusion, not to bootstrap the excluded** (Jim Crow). That is the loop running backwards in the real world. The mechanism meant to dissolve the hierarchy is held by the people who profit from it.

**(b) The incentive points the wrong way at *both* ends.** A heavier vote is a **status reward** — so the moment you grant it, you create a class with an interest in *keeping* the criterion that privileges them, not retiring it. Self-liquidation fights self-interest. And the people who must *provide* the education (incumbents) *lose* relative power as the loop runs — so they drag their feet. The carrot that's supposed to drive the loop forward also pays everyone to stall it.

**(c) "Educated" is not "votes better" — the evidence is worse than neutral.** Education raises competence on *factual* questions, but most political choices are about *values* (whose interests to weigh), and education doesn't make values more "correct" — [Arrow](../../proposals/beyond-democracy/frontier-mechanisms.md) says there's no correct aggregate ranking to be more right about. Worse, **education can amplify bias**: Kahan's *motivated numeracy* experiments found the **most** numerate partisans polarized **more**, not less — they used their extra skill to rationalize their tribe's position ([Kahan et al., *Behavioural Public Policy* 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/abs/motivated-numeracy-and-enlightened-selfgovernment/EC9F2410D5562EF10B7A5E2539063806)). So weighting by education doesn't reliably buy wiser collective judgment; it can buy more sophisticated motivated reasoning, tilted toward the credentialed class's interests.

**(d) It down-weights the people a decision affects most.** Welfare, policing, and schooling policy fall hardest on the poor and least-schooled — exactly those whose votes this would shrink. That is a consent-of-the-governed problem and it collides with the project's [rights floor](../../proposals/beyond-democracy/README.md): political equality isn't only instrumental, it's the statement that everyone's stake counts.

**The even-handed verdict (the owner's own test, applied honestly):** democracy's failure mode — electing a bad leader — is **reversible** (you can remove them; [speed-vs-reversibility](../../proposals/beyond-democracy/speed-vs-reversibility.md)). Weighted-voting's failure mode — capture of the "educated" criterion by the powerful — is **self-entrenching** (it rewrites the electorate in the captor's favour and compounds). Same single-failure test, opposite results. That, not Brennan's say-so, is why the weighting loses.

## 4. The salvage — keep the goal and the loop, change the engine

Per this project's method (steelman → improve), separate the **two things the owner actually wants** and deliver both *without* a franchise hierarchy:

- **Goal 1 — better-informed decisions.** Get it from **sortition + deliberation + revocable liquid delegation** ([frontier-mechanisms](../../proposals/beyond-democracy/frontier-mechanisms.md)): a random panel is *briefed by experts on the specific issue* before deciding, and anyone can *delegate* their vote to someone they trust on a topic. This puts knowledge where it matters **per issue**, by consent, with **nobody** disenfranchised and **no official defining "educated."** Note the owner's *best* variant — "you should have to understand the specific measure to vote on it" — converges here: issue-specific informed consent, done safely, *is* a deliberative mini-public.
- **Goal 2 — the education positive-loop.** Keep it! Just run it on **equal votes**: education as a constitutional right + outcome-funding. Educated populations *do* vote for more education — they never needed extra vote-weight to do it, and the status reward in (b) actually sabotages the loop. You get the virtuous cycle without handing anyone the criterion.

So the answer is not "drop your idea." It's: **the loop is good — keep it; the weighting is the wrong engine for it — it inverts the incentives and hands the powerful a dial to capture.** Build the loop on equal votes and deliberation, and it runs forward instead of backward.

## 5. The owner's stronger second proposal — weight by *domain*, not by general education

The owner then sharpened the idea, and it's now much better: **don't give "the educated" a blanket bonus — weight each person's vote *per issue*, by their *relevant* skill.** A doctor's vote counts ~100% on a vaccine law, ~20% on a computer law; a manual labourer's ~2% on each technical domain. And he caught a real inconsistency in my argument: *"no human is competent across all of government" applies to a president too.* **Correct** — competence-across-everything is impossible for *anyone*; a president only "solves" it by delegating to expert agencies. So that objection isn't unique to direct voting, and I shouldn't have leaned on it as if it were. Conceded.

**Why this beats §2's version:** it does **not** create one all-powerful "educated class." Power is split across domains — the doctor rules vaccines but is a near-layman on tax law. That defuses the worst objection to classic epistocracy (one credentialed class entrenching itself across *everything*).

**But it introduces a new flaw that is worse — and structural, not a matter of opinion:**

- **Someone must write the weight matrix *and classify every law into a domain* — and that someone is the real ruler.** Is a vaccine mandate a *medical* question (doctors win), an *economic* one (health-economists win), a *civil-liberties* one (everyone equal), or a *religious-freedom* one? It is all four at once. Whoever *classifies* it picks who wins — and the [McKelvey–Schofield theorem](../beyond-democracy/frontier-mechanisms.md) proves control of the agenda/classification *is* control of the outcome. You'd have built the most powerful capture surface in the system and labelled it "neutral expertise."
- **Most laws are value trade-offs, where no one is an expert.** A vaccine law is ~10% "what does immunology predict" and ~90% "how do we weigh one person's liberty against another's safety." A doctor has **no special authority on the 90%.** Weighting toward doctors silently decrees that the medical facts should dominate the value question — itself a political choice, and the exact technocratic overreach that breeds backlash.
- **It weights by *skill*, but the stake is *equal*.** The labourer cut to 2% is the one whose body takes the mandate, whose taxes pay for it, whose job it may cost. **Democracy weights by who is *affected*, not who *knows* — and on a mandate the labourer is affected 100%.** Skill-weighting gives the people a law lands on hardest the least say in it; and "2% on each domain" locks him out *everywhere* — the old disenfranchisement through a side door.

**The out-of-the-box part — two ways to keep your instinct and dodge the capture:**

1. **Split every law into its *facts* half and its *values* half — weight the first, equalise the second.** On "will this vaccine cut deaths, at what cost?" let competence weigh more. On "should we *mandate* it given the liberty cost?" it's one person, one vote, because there the labourer's stake equals the doctor's. This honours your insight (competence *should* weigh the technical part) while fixing the stake-vs-skill problem.
2. **Earn the weight by *track record*, not by *credential* — the bold version.** Don't assign weight from a diploma (capturable, class-correlated, static). Let domain-weight be **earned by being right** (measured against outcomes) and **lost by being wrong.** A self-taught person consistently correct on energy policy outweighs a credentialed economist consistently wrong; weight becomes *self-correcting* and *class-blind*, and no state body defines "educated" — results do. (It is [futarchy's](../beyond-democracy/frontier-mechanisms.md) logic applied to the *voter* instead of the *policy*.) **Honest limit:** it only works where outcomes are measurable and arrive in time — the *facts* half again; the *values* half has no "correct" outcome to score ([Arrow](../beyond-democracy/frontier-mechanisms.md)). So (1) and (2) combine: **earned weight on the measurable facts, equal votes on the values.**

And the deepest dodge — **AI digital twins** ([radical-options, Option 3](../beyond-democracy/radical-options.md)) make the weighting question *vanish*: instead of down-weighting the non-expert, raise *everyone* to expert-level per domain via their twin. Nobody is silenced; the competence gap closes from below instead of being enforced from above.

**The note you'll expect — but it matters:** your mandatory, state-set weight matrix is **liquid democracy minus one bit.** Make the weights *voluntary and self-assigned* — you delegate your vaccine vote to a doctor *you* trust, revocably — and you get the identical domain-weighting with **no matrix-writer to capture.** That single bit (who sets the weight: the state, or you) is the whole difference between a capture surface and a safe mechanism. Not a rival philosophy — *yours, with the bomb defused.*

## 6. Open question (logged, not closed)

Is there *any* capture-proof way to make a vote conditional on issue-specific understanding (not class-correlated "education") — e.g. a free, neutral, universally-passable "what does this measure actually do?" check — that doesn't collapse into "whoever writes the check controls the result"? If yes, it's a narrow, non-classist cousin of the owner's idea worth a real design. If no, sortition+deliberation is the safe form of the same instinct.
