# 07 · Food & Water Security

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

Whether every person can reliably get enough safe food and clean water. In scope: hunger and undernutrition, food-system resilience, safe drinking water, and sanitation. This is a problem of **distribution and access, not absolute scarcity** — the world produces enough calories for everyone today. Deeply tied to poverty (#01), climate (#04), health (#02), and conflict (#09).

## Key facts

- **~673 million people** faced hunger in 2024 (range 638–720M); **~2.3 billion** experienced moderate or severe food insecurity; **~2.6 billion** could not afford a healthy diet. *(FAO/SOFI, 2025)*
- **~2.1 billion people** still lack safely managed drinking water at home; **~3.4 billion** lack safely managed sanitation; **~354 million** still practice open defecation. *(WHO/UNICEF JMP, 2024)*
- The world **already grows enough food** to feed everyone — losses, waste, poverty, and conflict, not total supply, drive hunger.
- Roughly a **third of all food produced is lost or wasted** between farm and fork.
- Hunger is **rising in Africa and western Asia** even as it falls globally — progress is real but unevenly distributed, and conflict zones are the epicenter (link to #09).

## Root causes

- **Poverty.** Most hungry people could be fed if they could afford food; hunger is a symptom of #01 as much as of agriculture.
- **Conflict.** War destroys harvests, blocks aid, and is now the leading driver of acute food crises (link to #09).
- **Climate shocks.** Drought, floods, and heat cut yields and dry up water sources (link to #04).
- **Waste and broken supply chains.** A third of food is wasted while people starve — a logistics and incentive failure.
- **Under-investment in WASH.** Clean water and sanitation are cheap relative to their health payoff, yet chronically underfunded (link to #02).

## Connections

- **← #01 Poverty & #09 Conflict & #04 Climate:** the three biggest drivers of hunger.
- **→ #02 Health:** unsafe water and malnutrition are leading causes of preventable death, especially in children.
- **→ #03 Education:** hungry children can't learn; stunting impairs cognition for life.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **Income floors & social protection** (cash transfers, school meals). *For:* attacks hunger's root (poverty) directly and fast. *Objection:* recurring cost; needs functional governance to deliver.
2. **Cut food loss and waste.** *For:* feeds more with what we already grow; climate co-benefit. *Objection:* diffuse fixes across storage, transport, retail, and behavior.
3. **Resilient, sustainable agriculture** — better seeds, irrigation, soil health, smallholder support. *For:* raises yields where need is highest; buffers climate shocks. *Objection:* must avoid environmental harm; tech access is unequal.
4. **WASH investment** — safely managed water and sanitation for all. *For:* among the highest-return health investments known. *Objection:* infrastructure cost; maintenance and governance often neglected.
5. **Protect food systems from conflict** — humanitarian access, not weaponizing food. *For:* directly addresses the top driver of acute crises. *Objection:* depends on political will and #09.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Prevalence of undernourishment; number in moderate/severe food insecurity.
- Share who can afford a healthy diet.
- Population with safely managed drinking water and sanitation; open-defecation rate.
- Food loss/waste share; child stunting rate.

## Open questions

- How do we move from emergency food aid to durable income-based food security?
- Can agriculture scale to feed ~10 billion without wrecking climate and ecosystems (#04)?
- What's the fastest route to universal WASH given the infrastructure cost?
