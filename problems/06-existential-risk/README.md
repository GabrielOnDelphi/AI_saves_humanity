# 06 · Existential & Emerging-Technology Risk

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

The small set of risks that could kill a large fraction of humanity or permanently wreck our future: advanced AI, nuclear weapons, engineered pandemics, and other powerful dual-use technologies. These are **low-probability-per-year but unbounded-in-cost** — the asymmetry is the whole point. In scope: catastrophic and existential risks and their governance. (Climate is catastrophic too, but large enough to have its own folder, #04.)

## Key facts

- This is the **first era** in which a handful of human-made technologies could plausibly end modern civilization. That is historically new and changes the math of risk.
- **Nuclear arsenals** remain in the tens of thousands of warheads globally; arms-control treaties have been eroding, and the risk of use (intentional or accidental) is widely judged to have risen.
- **Engineered/enhanced pathogens**: advances in biotechnology lower the cost and skill needed to create dangerous pathogens — a deliberate or accidental pandemic could dwarf COVID-19.
- **Advanced AI**: rapidly increasing capability raises concerns from misuse (cyber, bio, disinformation) to loss-of-control of systems we don't fully understand. Expert opinion ranges widely, but a non-trivial fraction take catastrophic risk seriously.
- These risks **interact**: AI can accelerate bioweapon design; instability (#09) raises nuclear risk; all are worsened by weak global governance (#05).

## Root causes

- **Capability outrunning wisdom.** We can build powerful things faster than we can build the institutions to govern them safely.
- **Race dynamics.** Nations and companies fear being second, so they cut safety to move fast — a collective-action trap.
- **Diffuse, deferred, low-probability harm.** Markets and elections systematically under-invest in preventing rare catastrophes (link to #05's time-horizon problem).
- **Dual-use.** The same research that cures disease can create a weapon; you can't simply ban the knowledge.
- **Weak global coordination.** No effective body governs frontier AI or biotech the way nuclear non-proliferation (imperfectly) governs weapons.

## Connections

- **← #05 Governance:** without coordinated, far-sighted governance these risks are unmanaged.
- **↔ #09 Conflict:** great-power tension drives both nuclear risk and AI/bio arms races.
- **→ #10 Information:** AI supercharges disinformation, eroding the shared reality needed to respond to any crisis.
- **↔ #02 Health:** pandemic preparedness is the overlap with biosecurity.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **Frontier-AI governance** — safety testing, evaluations, compute/transparency requirements, incident reporting, international agreements. *For:* targets the fastest-moving risk before it's locked in. *Objection:* hard to define, enforce, and coordinate; risk of regulatory capture or stifling benefit.
2. **Biosecurity** — DNA-synthesis screening, pathogen-research oversight, far better detection and response. *For:* cheap relative to the downside; dual benefit with #02. *Objection:* governance lags the technology; enforcement across borders is weak.
3. **Nuclear de-escalation** — arms control, no-first-use norms, de-alerting, restored treaties. *For:* reduces the oldest existential risk. *Objection:* requires trust between rivals exactly when it's lowest (#09).
4. **Differential technology development** — speed up defensive/safety tech relative to offensive capability. *For:* shapes the trajectory rather than just braking. *Objection:* hard to steer; offense often cheaper than defense.
5. **Resilience & recovery** — food/seed/knowledge reserves, refuges, continuity planning. *For:* limits worst-case if prevention fails. *Objection:* easy to deprioritize; can breed false comfort.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Number of nuclear warheads; status of major arms-control treaties.
- Adoption of AI safety evaluations and binding governance among frontier labs/states.
- Coverage of DNA-synthesis screening; pathogen-detection lead time.
- Existence and authority of any credible international body for frontier tech.

## Open questions

- Can frontier AI be governed without either reckless acceleration or capture by incumbents?
- How do you regulate dual-use knowledge that can't be un-invented?
- How much should humanity spend now to prevent risks that *probably* won't materialize this decade — but would be irreversible if they did?
