# 12 · Human Rights, Freedom & Justice

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

Whether people are free, treated with dignity, and equal before the law. In scope: civil and political liberties, freedom from oppression and discrimination, the rule of law as *actually applied*, and justice for the powerless. This is the **moral floor** of the project — it also acts as a guardrail on every proposed solution: a "fix" that tramples rights isn't a fix. It is directly in tension with parts of the founding political proposal, which is why that proposal is stress-tested so hard (see [`proposals/political-system/`](../../proposals/political-system/)).

## Key facts

- A **majority of the world's population** lives under governments rated "not free" or only "partly free," and political rights and civil liberties have declined globally for much of the past two decades. *(Freedom House)*
- **Discrimination by gender, race, religion, caste, ethnicity, disability, and sexual orientation** remains pervasive, legally and informally.
- Hundreds of millions live in conditions of **forced labor, trafficking, or modern slavery**, and tens of millions are stateless or denied legal identity.
- **"Rule of law" is unevenly applied**: laws frequently bind the poor and protect the powerful — the inverse of justice (link to #01, #05).
- Journalists, dissidents, and human-rights defenders are **jailed or killed** in many countries for exposing abuse (link to #10).

## Root causes

- **Concentrated, unaccountable power.** Where power isn't checked (#05), rights are the first thing sacrificed.
- **Inequality.** Unequal wealth buys unequal justice; the poor can't afford the law (#01).
- **Discrimination and exclusion.** Entrenched hierarchies deny dignity and access to whole groups.
- **Weak or captured institutions.** Courts, police, and press that aren't independent become tools of the powerful rather than shields for the weak.
- **Impunity.** When abusers face no consequence, abuse is rational.

## Connections

- **↔ #05 Governance:** rights and accountable governance rise and fall together.
- **← #01 Inequality:** economic power converts into legal advantage.
- **→ #11 Mental health & #09 Conflict:** oppression and injustice cause trauma and fuel violence.
- **⚠ ↔ political proposal:** weighting votes by education (the founding idea) collides with equality before the law and political equality — a tension this folder exists to flag. The proposal folder addresses it directly.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **Independent courts, free press, and rights protections in law** (constitutional guarantees, judicial independence). *For:* the structural defense of everyone, including against majorities. *Objection:* requires the very independence that the powerful resist.
2. **Equal access to justice** — legal aid, simplified courts, anti-corruption in the justice system. *For:* makes rights real for the poor, not just on paper. *Objection:* underfunded; slow; resisted by beneficiaries of the status quo.
3. **Anti-discrimination law and enforcement.** *For:* expands dignity and participation. *Objection:* law alone doesn't change culture; enforcement gaps.
4. **Protect defenders & whistleblowers; international accountability.** *For:* keeps abuses visible and costly. *Objection:* weak international enforcement; sovereignty conflicts.
5. **Universal legal identity.** *For:* without it, people are invisible to services and rights. *Objection:* surveillance and exclusion risks if poorly designed.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Freedom/civil-liberties indices and their trend (Freedom House, V-Dem).
- Rule-of-law and access-to-justice indices (e.g., World Justice Project).
- Prevalence of forced labor/trafficking; number stateless or without legal identity.
- Imprisonment/killing of journalists and rights defenders.

## Open questions

- How are rights protected *against* democratic majorities as well as against autocrats?
- Can international human-rights enforcement be made real without overriding legitimate sovereignty?
- Where exactly is the line between a legitimate solution and one that sacrifices rights for results? *(This project treats that line as non-negotiable — solutions are judged against it.)*
