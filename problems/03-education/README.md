# 03 · Education & Learning

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

Whether people actually learn to read, reason, and keep learning — not just whether they sit in classrooms. In scope: foundational literacy/numeracy, school access, teaching quality, critical thinking, and lifelong/adult learning. Education is special on this list because it is **both a problem and a lever**: a population that can think clearly is the precondition for solving most of the others — and it is central to the founding political proposal (see [`proposals/political-system/`](../../proposals/political-system/)).

## Key facts

- About **6 in 10 children worldwide** cannot read and understand a simple, age-appropriate text by **age 10** ("learning poverty"). In low- and middle-income countries it's **more than half** of children. *(World Bank / UNESCO, 2024)*
- Hundreds of millions of children and youth are **out of school** entirely, concentrated in the poorest regions and among girls in conflict zones.
- The problem is **quality, not just access**: many children attend school for years and still can't read — "schooling without learning."
- Adult illiteracy remains in the hundreds of millions, disproportionately women.
- Even in rich countries, **critical-thinking and media-literacy gaps** leave populations vulnerable to misinformation (link to #10).

## Root causes

- **Poverty.** Children work, go hungry, or lack books, electricity, and a quiet place to study (links to #01, #07).
- **Teaching, not just teachers.** Shortage of trained teachers, but also weak methods, no feedback, and curricula that test memorization over understanding.
- **Foundational gaps compound.** A child who can't read by 10 falls further behind every year — early failure is rarely caught and fixed.
- **Inequity of access.** Gender, disability, language, rural location, and conflict all gate who learns.
- **Misaligned goals.** Systems optimize for credentials and exams rather than for the ability to think, adapt, and keep learning.

## Connections

- **→ Everything.** An educated, numerate, critically-thinking population is the substrate for better health choices (#02), functioning democracy (#05), resistance to misinformation (#10), and economic mobility (#01).
- **← #01 Inequality:** quality education is rationed by wealth, reproducing the class structure.
- **↔ #05/political-system:** the founding proposal would weight votes by education — which makes *fair access to education* a precondition for that proposal to be anything but a privilege amplifier. The proposal folder treats this head-on.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **"Foundational learning first"** — relentless focus on getting every child reading and doing basic math by age 10 (e.g., teaching at the right level, structured pedagogy). *For:* strong evidence, high return. *Objection:* unglamorous; competes with prestige projects for funding.
2. **Invest in teachers** — training, mentoring, pay, and respect. *For:* teacher quality is the biggest in-school factor. *Objection:* slow, expensive, politically diffuse.
3. **Target the excluded** — cash transfers/meals to keep poor kids in school; programs for girls, disabled, and conflict-affected children. *For:* directly removes access barriers. *Objection:* recurring cost; needs governance to reach the right people.
4. **Teach thinking, not just facts** — critical thinking, statistics, and media literacy as core curriculum. *For:* hardens society against #10. *Objection:* hard to measure; resisted by those who benefit from a credulous public.
5. **Open, free learning** — high-quality open educational resources, AI tutors, lifelong learning. *For:* scales cheaply; reaches adults. *Objection:* the digital divide can widen gaps; no substitute for teachers for the youngest learners.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Learning-poverty rate (share of 10-year-olds who can't read a simple text).
- Out-of-school numbers, disaggregated by gender, disability, and conflict status.
- Learning outcomes vs. years of schooling (are the years producing learning?).
- A civic/critical-thinking or media-literacy indicator.

## Open questions

- What's the fastest credible path from "in school" to "actually reading"?
- Can AI tutoring close gaps without deepening the digital divide?
- How do we measure critical thinking well enough to teach and fund it?
