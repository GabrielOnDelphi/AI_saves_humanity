# 01 · Economic Inequality & the Concentration of Power

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

The pooling of wealth — and the political power that wealth buys — in the hands of a very small number of people and corporations. In scope: wealth/income concentration, tax systems, inheritance, monopoly power, and the feedback loop between money and political influence. Out of scope (covered elsewhere): poverty's effect on health (#02), education access (#03), and democratic capture mechanics (#05).

This is arguably the **upstream problem**: extreme inequality of wealth becomes inequality of power, which then blocks the fixes for almost everything else on this list.

## Key facts

- The richest **1% own ~43.8%** of all global wealth. The poorest **half of humanity owns ~0.52%**. On average a person in the top 1% holds **~8,251×** the wealth of someone in the bottom 50%. *(Oxfam, 2025)*
- Billionaire wealth rose **>16% in 2025** to a record **~$18.3 trillion**, and the number of billionaires passed **3,000** for the first time. *(Oxfam, 2025)*
- The **top 10% of earners take home more than the other 90% combined**; fewer than **60,000 people** control three times the wealth of the bottom half of humanity. *(World Inequality Report 2026)*
- **~817 million people** lived in extreme poverty in 2024 (under the updated $3/day line). *(World Bank, 2025)*
- Billionaires are **~4,000× more likely to hold political office** than an average citizen — the clearest sign that money and power have merged. *(Oxfam, 2025)*

## Root causes

- **Returns on capital outpace wages.** When owning assets grows wealth faster than working does, anyone who starts with assets pulls away, and the gap compounds across generations.
- **Inheritance without limit.** Dynastic wealth transfers concentration across time; much top wealth is inherited, not earned.
- **Tax systems that favor capital over labor.** Wages are taxed more reliably than capital gains, and the largest fortunes are often taxed least (borrow-against-assets strategies, offshore structures).
- **Monopoly and market power.** Concentrated industries extract rents; weak antitrust lets winners entrench.
- **The money→power→money loop.** Wealth funds lobbying, campaigns, and media, which shape rules (tax, antitrust, labor) to protect wealth. This is the engine that makes the problem self-reinforcing and is why it sits upstream of #05.

## Connections

- **→ #05 Governance:** concentrated wealth captures policy; this is the master link.
- **→ #02 Health / #03 Education:** the poor get worse care and schooling, entrenching the gap.
- **→ #10 Information:** concentrated media ownership shapes what people believe.
- **→ #09 Conflict / #11 Social fragmentation:** extreme inequality corrodes trust and fuels unrest.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **Progressive wealth & inheritance taxation.** *For:* directly targets concentration; funds public goods. *Objection:* capital flight, valuation difficulty, enforcement; needs international coordination to work.
2. **Close the capital/labor tax gap & end offshore secrecy.** *For:* large revenue, harder to dodge with a global minimum + automatic info exchange. *Objection:* requires multilateral agreement; powerful resistance.
3. **Antitrust / break up monopolies.** *For:* restores competition, lowers rents, disperses power. *Objection:* slow, litigation-heavy; risk of capture by the regulated.
4. **Predistribution** — broaden who owns capital from the start: employee ownership, sovereign/citizen wealth funds, baby bonds. *For:* fixes the source, not just the symptom. *Objection:* slow to compound; politically hard.
5. **Floor, not just ceiling** — guaranteed income / negative income tax. *For:* removes the worst deprivation now. *Objection:* cost; doesn't by itself stop concentration at the top.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Top-1% and top-0.1% wealth share; bottom-50% share.
- Ratio of capital income to labor income, and effective tax rate by wealth percentile.
- Number of people in extreme poverty.
- A "power" metric: share of campaign finance / lobbying spend from the top 0.1%.

## Open questions

- Is there a level of inequality that's *acceptable* (rewards effort) versus *corrosive* (buys the rules)? Where's the line?
- Can any single country act alone, or does every effective lever require international coordination?
- Predistribution vs. redistribution — which is more durable against capture?
