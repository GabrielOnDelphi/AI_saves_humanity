# 05 · Governance, Democracy & Corruption

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

Whether power is accountable to the people it governs — or captured by the few. In scope: democratic design and decline, corruption, state capacity, rule of law's *application*, and the money→power loop. This is the **decision-making layer**: almost every other problem is gated by whether we can make and enforce good collective choices. It is also where the project's founding proposal lives — a redesign of how we elect leaders (see [`proposals/political-system/`](../../proposals/political-system/)).

## Key facts

- Billionaires are **~4,000× more likely to hold political office** than ordinary citizens — a direct measurement of money converting into power. *(Oxfam, 2025)*
- Democracy has been in measured decline for much of the past two decades; a majority of the world's people now live under regimes rated "not free" or only "partly free." *(Freedom House; V-Dem)*
- Trust in government and institutions has fallen across many democracies, weakening the consent that makes governance work (link to #11).
- Corruption diverts an estimated **trillions of dollars** a year from public goods into private pockets — money that could fund #02, #03, #07.
- Many governments are **technically democratic but practically captured**: elections happen, but agendas are set by donors, lobbies, and concentrated media (#01, #10).

## Root causes

- **The money→power loop.** Wealth funds campaigns, lobbying, and media; the rules it buys protect wealth. This is the master mechanism linking #01 and #05.
- **Misaligned time horizons.** Electoral cycles reward short-term visible wins; the biggest problems (#04, #02 prevention, #06) pay off later.
- **Voter information limits.** Citizens are rationally ignorant — they have little incentive to study every issue — leaving room for manipulation (#10). *(This is the real problem the founding proposal tries to answer.)*
- **Institutional design flaws.** Winner-take-all systems, gerrymandering, and weak checks let minorities rule or majorities tyrannize.
- **Weak rule of law.** Laws that bind the poor but not the powerful destroy legitimacy (link to #12).

## Connections

- **↔ #01 Inequality:** the two reinforce each other; together they form the upstream block on everything else.
- **→ #04 Climate / #02 Health / #03 Education:** captured or short-termist governance underfunds long-term public goods.
- **← #10 Information:** a misinformed electorate can't hold power accountable.
- **↔ #12 Rights & justice:** governance sets whether law protects or oppresses.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **Get money out of politics** — campaign-finance limits, transparency, public/"democracy voucher" funding, lobbying restrictions. *For:* attacks the master loop directly. *Objection:* the captured won't legislate their own constraints; free-speech tensions.
2. **Deliberative democracy** — citizens' assemblies chosen by **sortition** (random selection), deliberative polling, participatory budgeting. *For:* raises decision *quality* without disenfranchising anyone; tested and effective on hard issues. *Objection:* advisory power only unless institutionalized; scale.
3. **Electoral reform** — proportional representation, ranked-choice voting, independent redistricting. *For:* better represents actual preferences; reduces wasted votes. *Objection:* can fragment governments; entrenched parties resist.
4. **Anti-corruption infrastructure** — independent courts, free press, audit bodies, asset disclosure, whistleblower protection. *For:* recovers public money; rebuilds trust. *Objection:* requires the very independence corruption erodes.
5. **Rethink the vote itself** — the founding proposal (education-weighted direct election) and its alternatives (liquid democracy, competence floors, expert-execution-under-democratic-mandate). *For:* tries to fix the voter-information root. *Objection:* serious risk of entrenching privilege — see the proposal folder for the full, honest fight over this.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Democracy/freedom indices (V-Dem, Freedom House) and their trend.
- Corruption-perception and actual-bribery measures.
- Share of political funding from the top 0.1%.
- Trust in institutions; voter turnout *and* informedness.
- Gap between law-on-paper and law-as-applied to the powerful.

## Open questions

- Can the money→power loop be broken from *inside* a captured system, or only after crisis?
- Does raising decision quality require changing *who* votes (risky) or *how* we deliberate (safer)? The project leans toward the latter — argued out in the proposal folder.
- How do we keep reforms from being captured by whoever implements them?
