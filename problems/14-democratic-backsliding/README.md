# 14 · Democratic Backsliding (How Democracies Slide into Autocracy)

**Status:** Draft for debate · **Last updated:** 2026-06-29

> Added at the owner's request: *"how to prevent a country from turning from democracy to dictatorship (see America under Trump)."* This is the **defensive** half of the transition problem — Door 2 in [proposal 00](../../proposals/00-how-change-happens/). Closely tied to [#05 Governance](../05-governance-democracy/); kept separate because *losing* a democracy and *never having had one* are different problems with different cures. The concrete defense lives in [`proposals/defending-democracy`](../../proposals/defending-democracy/).

## Scope

How established democracies decay into authoritarian rule — and how that decay is detected, slowed, and reversed. In scope: the mechanics of backsliding (capture of referee institutions, erosion of norms), early-warning signs, and structural defenses. Out of scope: building democracy from scratch in an autocracy (that's [#05](../05-governance-democracy/) and [proposal 00, Doors 1 & 3](../../proposals/00-how-change-happens/)).

## Key facts

- **Global freedom has declined for 20 consecutive years.** Just **21%** of the world's people live in countries rated "Free," down from **46%** two decades ago; in the latest year 54 countries deteriorated and only 35 improved. The freedoms hit hardest over 20 years are **media freedom, personal expression, and due process**. *(Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2026.)*
- **Autocracies now outnumber democracies** — 91 to 88 — for the first time in over two decades, and nearly a quarter of the world's nations are actively autocratizing. *(V-Dem, Democracy Report 2026.)*
- **Modern democracies rarely die by coup.** They are dismantled from within by **elected** leaders who slowly capture the referees (courts, election officials, prosecutors, media regulators) and then tilt the field while still holding elections. *(Levitsky & Ziblatt, How Democracies Die, 2018.)*
- **The decline reached the established West.** Six of the ten newly-autocratizing countries in V-Dem's 2026 report are in Europe and North America — including Italy, the UK, and the **United States**, the case the owner flagged. *(V-Dem, Democracy Report 2026.)*

## The U.S. case — applied carefully, non-partisan, primary-sourced

This is the part the owner specifically asked for. The discipline here is strict: **report what credentialed measurement institutions have documented, with figures and links — not opinion.** Three bodies that use *completely different methods* are the backbone, and they converge:

- **Freedom House** (analysts code a fixed checklist of laws and events): the U.S. scored **81/100 in the 2026 report, still rated "Free," but down from 84** the year before — one of the larger one-year drops among established democracies.
- **V-Dem** (≈4,000 country experts rate hundreds of indicators): the U.S. **Liberal Democracy Index fell ~24% in a single year**, and its world rank dropped from **20th to 51st of 179** — which V-Dem calls a faster deterioration than any other democracy in modern times. *(Democracy Report 2026, released 2026-03-17.)*
- **Bright Line Watch** (panel survey of U.S. political scientists): experts' rating of U.S. democracy fell from **67/100 (Nov 2024) to 54/100 (Sept 2025)** — between Israel (49) and Mexico (60), far below the UK (83) or Canada (88).

**Why "convergence" matters:** any one index can be biased or gamed. Three instruments with different builders, methods, and failure modes pointing the same way in the same window is the strongest signal available — and the hardest to wave off as partisan.

**Mapping the documented actions to [Levitsky & Ziblatt's four warning signs](#root-causes).** Each item below is sourced to a measurement body, not asserted here:

1. **Rejecting the democratic rules of the game** — the executive **withheld funds Congress had appropriated and spent/raised funds without congressional approval**, issued **220+ executive orders** in 2025 frequently invoking emergency powers, and **defied or resisted court oversight in over a third of cases decided against it**; V-Dem summarizes the term as "a rapid concentration of powers in the presidency." *(Freedom House 2026 U.S. report; V-Dem 2026.)*
2. **Denying the legitimacy of opponents** — expert confidence that government agencies are *not* used to punish opponents **collapsed from 68% to 7%** (Nov 2024 → Sept 2025); Freedom House documents removal of **18 inspectors general**, firing the head of the Office of Special Counsel, and cutting the DOJ's public-corruption unit. *(Bright Line Watch; Freedom House 2026.)*
3. **Tolerating or encouraging violence** — political violence rose as a **cross-partisan** danger: the Sept 10 2025 assassination of conservative activist Charlie Kirk (85% of experts called it a threat to democracy), alongside National Guard deployments to U.S. cities (77% rated a serious threat). Notably the data cut *both* ways — a small but non-trivial share on the left, asked directly, approved of the Kirk killing — which is exactly why this sign must be applied to *all* sides. *(Bright Line Watch, Sept 2025.)*
4. **Curtailing civil liberties of opponents and the press** — Congress **defunded the Corporation for Public Broadcasting**; press-freedom violations and arrests of journalists rose; the administration moved to **punish nonviolent speech by noncitizens**; expert confidence in press freedom fell from **76% to 23%**. *(Freedom House 2026; Bright Line Watch.)*

**The honest caveats, stated up front:**
- The U.S. is **not a dictatorship** — it is a backsliding democracy still rated "Free." Naming the trend is not claiming the destination has been reached. The whole point of an early-warning framework is to act *before* the destination.
- Levitsky & Ziblatt's test was built to spot autocrats *before* they take power. Applying it to a sitting administration is a harder, more contested exercise — so the safeguard here is to lean on **third-party measurement**, not narration.
- The **partisan perception gap is itself a finding**: Republicans rate U.S. democracy at 58.5, Democrats at 43.6 — the widest split Bright Line Watch has recorded. How healthy you think the system is depends heavily on your team, and that polarization is the accelerant described below.
- The framework is **content-neutral**: it would flag the same behaviors from any party. That neutrality is what keeps this analysis, not advocacy.

## Root causes

- **Referee capture.** Whoever controls courts, election administration, prosecution, and oversight controls outcomes. Capture these and you can win while *appearing* to follow the rules.
- **Norm erosion.** Levitsky & Ziblatt identify two unwritten norms that keep democracies alive: **mutual toleration** (treating rivals as legitimate, not enemies) and **forbearance** (not using every legal power to its maximum destructive extent). Both erode before institutions visibly fall.
- **The four warning signs** (Levitsky & Ziblatt): a leader (1) rejects or weakly commits to democratic rules of the game, (2) denies the legitimacy of opponents, (3) tolerates or encourages violence, (4) shows readiness to curtail the civil liberties of opponents and the media. Any *one* is a red flag.
- **Polarization as accelerant.** When each side sees the other as an existential threat, voters tolerate anti-democratic behavior from "their" side — the immune system that should reject autocrats stops working. The redistricting arms race (Texas redrew maps mid-decade for ~5 seats; California answered in kind) is the mechanism in miniature: each defection justifies the next.
- **Upstream pressure from [#01](../01-economic-inequality/) and [#10](../10-information-truth/):** concentrated wealth funds capture; algorithmic information chaos makes the legitimacy attacks land.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

> **Full proposal: [`proposals/defending-democracy`](../../proposals/defending-democracy/)** — how to insulate referees, codify forbearance, and pre-commit a civil-society response *before* the crisis, plus the transition path for each.

1. **Insulate the referees.** Independent election administration, fixed/staggered judicial terms, non-partisan prosecution, protected inspectors-general. *Objection:* "independent" bodies can themselves be captured slowly; independence needs its own guardians.
2. **Harden norms into rules** where possible — codify forbearance: limit emergency powers, cap pardon abuse, bind the spending power to appropriations, ban mid-decade gerrymanders. *Objection:* you cannot codify every norm; over-rigid rules invite legalistic evasion.
3. **Depolarize / restore mutual toleration.** *For:* attacks the accelerant. *Objection:* nobody has a reliable method; it may require electoral-system change (proportional representation lowers winner-take-all stakes).
4. **Pre-committed civil-society response** — the Door-2 application of [Chenoweth's findings](../../proposals/00-how-change-happens/): broad, nonviolent, disciplined non-cooperation is the empirically strongest brake on a power grab, *if* it is organized in advance. *Objection:* movements are reactive and slow; by the time the threat is undeniable the tools to resist may already be gone — which is the argument for building the response now.

## Metrics we'd watch

- V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index and Freedom House score, **trend** (slope, not just level) — the U.S. one-year slope above is the alarm, not the absolute 81.
- Independence of courts and election bodies; rate of executive compliance with adverse court rulings.
- Press-freedom indices; ratio of prosecutions of political opponents vs. allies.
- The "four warning signs" checklist applied to the sitting head of government — **dated, sourced, and applied identically regardless of party.**

## Open questions

- What is the **earliest reliable signal** of backsliding — before it's irreversible? (The three indices above only turned sharply *after* the term began; can anything lead the event?)
- Does proportional representation actually reduce backsliding risk, or just change its shape?
- Can a captured judiciary or election authority ever be **un-captured** without that reversal itself becoming a partisan purge?
- Is the U.S. decline a **reversible swing** (it has recovered before) or a **ratchet** that locks in? The difference decides how much Door 2 effort is worth.
