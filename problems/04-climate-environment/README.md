# 04 · Climate Change & Environmental Collapse

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-06-16

## Scope

The destabilization of the systems that keep Earth livable: a warming climate, collapsing biodiversity, pollution, and the degradation of water, soil, and air. In scope: greenhouse-gas emissions, warming impacts, biodiversity loss, pollution, and the political economy that blocks action. Tightly coupled to energy (#08), food/water (#07), health (#02), and conflict (#09).

## Key facts

- **2024 was the first calendar year more than 1.5°C above the pre-industrial (1850–1900) baseline — about 1.55°C.** *(WMO, Jan 2025)*
- There's an **~86% chance** at least one of 2025–2029 exceeds 1.5°C, and a **~70% chance** the five-year average does. *(WMO, 2025)* Crossing 1.5°C in single years doesn't yet mean the long-term Paris goal is lost — but the margin is nearly gone.
- We are in a **mass biodiversity decline**: wildlife populations and insect abundance are falling sharply, undermining pollination, fisheries, and ecosystem stability.
- **Air pollution** contributes to millions of premature deaths a year (link to #02) — a present-tense killer, not just a future risk.
- Climate impacts (heat, drought, floods, sea-level rise) hit the **poorest hardest** despite their having emitted least (link to #01) — the core injustice of the problem.

## Root causes

- **A fossil-fuel-based energy system** (see #08) with a century of built infrastructure and political entrenchment.
- **Externalized costs.** Emitters don't pay for the damage they cause, so markets overproduce it. No global price on carbon.
- **Short time horizons.** Costs are now; the worst damage is later — a structural mismatch with electoral and quarterly cycles (link to #05).
- **Concentrated incumbent power.** Fossil interests fund doubt and lobby against transition (links to #01, #10).
- **Collective-action problem.** No single country's cuts solve it; everyone is tempted to free-ride.

## Connections

- **↔ #08 Energy:** the climate problem is largely an energy problem; solve clean energy and most of this follows.
- **→ #07 Food/Water:** warming drives drought, crop failure, and water stress.
- **→ #09 Conflict:** resource scarcity and climate migration raise conflict risk.
- **← #05 Governance & #01 Power:** capture and short-termism block action.

## Candidate solutions (with trade-offs)

1. **Price carbon** (tax or cap-and-trade), ideally with revenue returned to citizens. *For:* economy-wide, efficient signal. *Objection:* politically toxic if it raises living costs; needs border adjustments to prevent leakage.
2. **Crash deployment of clean energy & electrification** (see #08). *For:* costs have fallen fast; co-benefits for health and energy access. *Objection:* grids, storage, minerals, and permitting bottlenecks.
3. **Protect and restore ecosystems** — forests, wetlands, oceans. *For:* cheap carbon storage plus biodiversity. *Objection:* permanence and monitoring are hard; can't substitute for cutting emissions.
4. **Adaptation & resilience** — seawalls, drought-resistant crops, heat planning. *For:* unavoidable; protects the vulnerable now. *Objection:* expensive; the poorest can least afford it (justice/finance gap).
5. **Industrial decarbonization & R&D** — steel, cement, shipping, aviation, carbon removal. *For:* tackles the hard-to-abate ~30%. *Objection:* immature, costly tech; removal must not become an excuse to keep emitting.

## Metrics we'd watch

- Global GHG emissions (annual, and cumulative budget remaining for 1.5/2°C).
- Global mean temperature anomaly vs. 1850–1900.
- Share of clean energy in electricity and final energy.
- Biodiversity indicators (e.g., Living Planet Index); air-pollution deaths.
- Climate finance flowing to the most-affected, least-responsible countries.

## Open questions

- Can we decarbonize fast enough *without* coercion that breaks democratic consent (#05)?
- How much should we rely on carbon removal vs. treating it as a dangerous excuse?
- How is the burden split fairly between high historical emitters and developing nations?
